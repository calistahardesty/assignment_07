This assignment taught me how to scrape websites that were made for people,
not programs. Each café's website was built differently, so each scraper
needed a different trick. For Lakeshore Lattes, I used query_selector_all and
the ~ sibling selector to find the grid of cards after each section heading.
The cards had extra stuff like NEW! badges and V and GF tags mixed in with the
prices, so my cleaning functions had to take those out and split lines like
"S $5.05 · L $6.25" into separate sizes and prices. For Hilltop Grind,
pd.read_html read every table on the page at once. I used zip to pair each
table with its h2 heading, and melt to turn the S, M, and L columns into rows.

Canal Street was the hardest because nothing was in the HTML until the page's
JavaScript loaded it. My scraper had to pick a shop with select_option, click
Show full menu, wait for the cards, and keep clicking Show more until the
button went away. I learned that query_selector_all doesn't wait, so if you
call it too early it just returns an empty list without an error. I also
learned to save all the links first before visiting them, because leaving the
page gets rid of the cards you were looping through.

The matching part showed me that fuzzy matching measures spelling, not
meaning. Matcha Latte scored 87 against Mocha Latte, and the review page even
picked Mocha Latte for me, but they are totally different drinks. PSL scored
really low against Pumpkin Spice Latte but is the same drink. That's why the
code only matches things automatically at 90 or higher and asks a person about
the rest. This is called keeping a human in the loop. The review page showed
18 items, but I only had to answer 12, because once I named something like
Matcha Latte, the other cafés' versions matched it automatically.

I made one mistake during the review. I marked Hilltop's Egg & Cheese
Breakfast Sandwich as a new item, but it is actually our Breakfast Sandwich
with a longer name. All the tests still passed, but my Analysis page showed
26 prices below the market and 9 above instead of 25 and 10. I compared my
answers to the correct ones with diff, found the wrong line, and fixed it by
hand in data/item_matches.csv. This showed me that the analysis is only as
good as the matching, and that passing tests doesn't always mean the results
are right.

For the analysis, price_table pivots the prices so each café gets its own
column, compare_prices figures out where our price stands, and gap_analysis
uses an outer merge with indicator=True to find what only we sell and what
only the rivals sell.
